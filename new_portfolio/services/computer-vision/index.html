    Computer vision development services

    Vision projects succeed or fail on the quality of the data and the testing, not on model names. I start from your footage or images, agree how success is measured and say plainly what the numbers do and do not show.

    I offer computer vision development services: software that finds, counts or sorts things in photos and video. Examples are crowd counting, spotting events in camera footage and matching faces.     FAQ

    Questions about computer vision projects

    What is YOLO object detection?

    YOLO, short for You Only Look Once, is a fast method that finds and labels many objects in an image or video frame. It works in one pass and draws a box around each. I have used the YOLOv8 version for detection. It suits live counting and alerts, though accuracy still has to be tested on your own footage.

    How accurate is computer vision?

    I will not promise a number before I see your data. I measure on data the model has not seen and state exactly how it was split. My deepfake detector reports 74.16% on a random frame-level split, and says what that does not prove.

    How much does a computer vision project cost?

    It depends on how much footage or how many images you have, how much labelling is needed. The accuracy you need and whether it must run live also matter. I do not publish a price list. After a short brief I suggest a fixed price or hourly billing for a clear scope and agree it in writing before I start.

    Can you train a model on my own images or video?

    Yes, for small to medium sets of images. I trained a neural network on frames from a public deepfake dataset. I have also used ready-made detectors such as Faster R-CNN, YOLOv8n and a Vision Transformer. During planning we check how much labelled data you have and whether a ready-made model is the better start.

    Can it work on live camera feeds?

    My monitoring prototype checks video frames on a schedule, one every three seconds for the crime module, and raises alerts. Whether your setup needs true real-time speed depends on camera quality, the number of cameras and your hardware. We would test that during planning rather than assume it.

    What about privacy for face or surveillance projects?

    Those projects need consent, clear rules on how long data is kept and legal review on your side. I raise privacy questions during planning and can build in options such as limited data retention and restricted access to stored images. The legal decisions stay with you.
