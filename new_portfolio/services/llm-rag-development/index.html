    Freelance service

    Freelance AI chatbot developer for document assistants

    Many chatbot demos break on the first question the documents do not cover. Mine check the evidence first, name the source they used and admit when they cannot find an answer.

    As a freelance AI chatbot developer, I build Artificial Intelligence (AI) chatbots that answer from your own documents. Every answer shows its source, and when the documents do not contain the answer, the chatbot says so instead of guessing. The method is called RAG (retrieval-augmented generation). You receive a working service, a simple chat screen, tests and handover notes.

    Who it is for

    Who this is for

    • Support and operations leads

      Staff and customers keep asking questions whose answers already sit in manuals and policy files. A chatbot that answers from those files and shows its source spares people the search, and anyone can check the answer.

    • Founders adding an AI feature

      You want a chatbot or assistant inside a product or website. A working prototype on your own content shows real answer quality early, before you decide how much to build.

    • Compliance and HR teams

      Policies change and exact wording matters. You need each answer tied to the document it came from, and a plain "not found" when the policy says nothing on the question.

    • Teams with a customer chat channel

      Customers already write to you on WhatsApp or in an app. You need AI replies that do not keep them waiting, a saved history of each conversation and alerts for human operators.

    Problems I solve

    Problems I solve

    • ProblemNobody can tell where an answer came from

      How I approach itEach reply names the source document, shows the passage it relied on and carries a confidence label. A reviewer checks the claim against that passage instead of trusting the chatbot's wording.

    • ProblemThe bot misreads what the user is asking for

      How I approach itI design flows around what the user wants. A simple router sends document questions, booking requests and small talk to different handlers, and chat history lives in a database. In client chatbot work this reduced failures during complex bookings.

    • ProblemA hosted AI model is retired or slows down

      How I approach itModel names and fallback order live in settings, so the service switches to a backup model without a code change, and each reply states which model answered. Request IDs plus metrics and traces, as built in my Traceable AI Backend, make a silent switch visible.

    • ProblemYou cannot tell if a change made answers better or worse

      How I approach itI keep a list of questions with the expected answers, including ones the documents should not answer. My healthcare example has such a list, checked by hand; for a client I add automatic scoring on every change.

    • ProblemVisitors try to trick or flood the chatbot

      How I approach itOn the scam honeypot I added a filter for prompt-injection attempts (messages that try to override the AI's instructions) and a request limit of 1 per second per visitor, with bursts of 5. For a client I would add a test set of attacks.

    Technology

    Technology, and where it fits

    FastAPI and Pydantic
    Serve the chatbot through an API, check every request, and keep thresholds and model names in settings files.
    sentence-transformers
    Turns passages and questions into numbers that capture meaning, so the closest passages can be found. Runs on a standard processor (CPU).
    ChromaDB
    Stores those numbers on disk and returns the closest passages for each question.
    AI model APIs (Groq, OpenAI)
    The language model that writes each answer from the passages it is given, with backup models if one fails.
    Local AI models (Ollama, vLLM)
    Used instead of an online service when documents and prompts must stay on your own servers.
    PostgreSQL and Supabase
    Keep chat history and user profiles, so a bot can remember each conversation.
    Redis and Celery
    Run slow AI replies in the background, so a chat channel such as WhatsApp is not blocked while the AI works.
    Streamlit or React
    Provide the simple chat screen where reviewers see the answer, its source passage and the confidence label.
    Docker and pytest
    Docker packages the service so it runs the same anywhere. pytest runs the automatic checks after each change.

    Decide together

    Trade-offs we decide together

    • Online AI service or your own servers

      Online services such as Groq or OpenAI are quick to start. Hosting the model on your own servers keeps every document and prompt in-house but needs a stronger machine. I usually prototype on non-sensitive samples, then decide with you.

    • Strict answers or more answers

      A high match score gives more "not found" replies and fewer wrong answers. A low score answers more questions but risks mistakes. For policy, health or finance content I suggest starting strict and loosening only after testing real questions.

    • Remember the conversation or not

      Memory lets a user ask a follow-up, but it needs stored history, privacy rules and more testing. My healthcare example answers each question on its own. For customer chat I store history in a database. I add memory only when the use case needs it.

    FAQ

    Questions about chatbot projects

    What is a RAG chatbot?

    A RAG chatbot looks up the right passages in your own documents first and only then writes its answer from them. RAG stands for retrieval-augmented generation. Because the answer is built from your documents, it can show its source. It can also say "not found" when the documents do not cover the question, instead of guessing.

    How do you stop a chatbot from making things up?

    With several layers, not one instruction. First the chatbot checks that the documents really contain the answer. Weak matches never reach the AI language model (LLM), the part that writes the reply. A fixed fallback sentence covers the rest. In my healthcare example, out-of-scope questions get a plain "not found" instead of a guess.

    Can a chatbot run on my own servers?

    Often, yes. At work I build on AI models hosted on the client's own servers, so no data or prompts leave the building. My personal projects use online services such as Groq. During planning we choose what fits your privacy needs, budget and answer quality.

    How much does an AI chatbot cost for a website?

    I do not publish a price list, because chatbots vary a lot. The cost depends on how many documents you have, how they are organised, where the chatbot must run and how much testing you need. After reading your brief I suggest a fixed price or hourly billing. We agree it in writing before I start.

    What kinds of documents can the chatbot use?

    Text-based documents such as policies, manuals, circulars and support articles. Tables and images inside documents can be harder, so we test those early. I start with a small sample of yours to check how well the chatbot finds the right passages, before we commit to the full build.

    Can my team maintain it after you finish?

    That is the aim. Instructions and adjustable settings live in simple settings files, and the code has tests. The handover includes a walkthrough of how to add documents and tune the settings. If you want ongoing help, we can agree a maintenance period in writing.

    What happens when our documents change?

    The search index is rebuilt from the updated files. In my healthcare example, an ingest step can wipe and rebuild the index, and identical passages are not stored twice. It does not yet watch for file changes or keep old versions apart. For a client I would add both, so outdated wording cannot answer a new question.

    Does the chatbot need training on our documents?

    No training run is needed. When a question arrives, the chatbot searches your documents for the best passages, so updating it means changing the files and refreshing the search index, not retraining a model. In my language model projects, quality came from retrieval, checks and prompts, not from training a model.
