    FAQ

    Choosing a service

    Which AI development service do I need?

    Start from the problem. If people need answers from your documents, choose the chatbot service. To pull data out of PDFs, choose document AI. For apps and APIs choose Python backend. For photos and video choose computer vision. For audio or text models choose machine learning. For repeated tasks choose automation.

    Can a single build combine several of these services?

    Yes, and many builds do. A document assistant needs a backend API, and a WhatsApp bot may need AI replies and background jobs. I scope the work around your outcome, not around service labels, and list which parts belong to which area so you can see what is included.

    What if my idea does not fit any of these services?

    Send a short brief anyway. These are the areas where I have built things and can show evidence, but many problems cross several of them. If the work is outside what I do well, I will say so plainly and suggest what to ask for elsewhere, rather than take it on.

    Do I need clean, labelled data before an AI project can start?

    No. Looking at what you have is part of the first step. I check a small sample of your documents, recordings, images or tables and tell you early what is usable, what needs cleaning and whether you need more examples. Labelled data matters most for the machine learning work.

    Can I start with a small test before a full build?

    Yes. A working prototype on a sample of your own data is the usual first step. You see real quality early and decide with evidence whether to continue. We agree the scope and deadline for the prototype before work begins, and a full build can follow as a separate agreed step.

    Is my idea suited to AI, or would simple software do?

    Not every task needs AI. If fixed rules solve it, I will say so and suggest simple software, which is easier to run and to check. AI fits when the input is messy, such as free text, speech, images or long documents, and a measured result matters.
