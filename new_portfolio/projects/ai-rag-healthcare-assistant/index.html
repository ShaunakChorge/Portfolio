    Healthcare RAG chatbot that cites its sources

    Cited answers, or an honest "not found".

    This healthcare RAG chatbot answers questions from six made-up healthcare policy documents. RAG means it looks up the right passages first and then writes the answer from them. It returns its source and a confidence label, and refuses out-of-scope questions instead of guessing.

    Overview

    Why this matters

    Clinics, hospital groups, insurers and health-software teams keep their rules in long documents: appointment rules, refill limits, discharge instructions, privacy policy and billing terms. Staff and patients ask the same questions again and again. A chatbot can answer them, but in healthcare it is only useful if people can trust what it says and check where it came from.

    A good result answers only from your own documents and shows the document and passage behind each answer. When the documents are silent, it says "not found" and points the person to the facility instead of guessing. Booking requests go to a scheduling tool (a mock in this prototype), and the prompts are plain text that a non-programmer can review. This project shows that behaviour on made-up policies.

    The story

    The problem: policy answers buried in documents

    The problem

    Healthcare answers must come from the right document. A confident wrong answer is worse than no answer. Policies are long, staff and patients ask about them again and again, and a wrong answer can mean a missed rule, a complaint or a compliance problem. An answer with no source also cannot be checked, so a reviewer cannot trust it.

    What I built

    I built the chatbot as a small FastAPI service with three safety layers. First, a router works out what the user wants: a booking request, small talk or a policy question. Second, for policy questions the six documents are searched by meaning, and weak matches are dropped before the AI is called. Third, a strict instruction forces an exact fallback sentence when nothing relevant remains. Each answer drawn from the documents returns its source, a short excerpt and a confidence label. Settings and prompts live in plain files, and a chain of backup AI models keeps the service running if one is retired.

    What made it hard

    1. 01

      Wrong answers cost more

      In a healthcare setting a confident wrong answer is worse than no answer, so the chatbot must show its source and be able to refuse.

    2. 02

      Telling questions apart

      Booking requests, small talk and policy questions need different handling. A question such as "how are telehealth visits billed?" starts like small talk but is a policy question.

    3. 03

      Keeping the AI inside the documents

      A language model uses what it already knows unless told not to. The prompt had to forbid outside knowledge and medical advice, and weak matches had to be stopped before the model saw them.

    4. 04

      Hosted models get retired

      The settings file records two of the models I used as deprecated by the provider. The service needs a backup list that can change without code edits.

    Context and credits

    • It was a take-home prototype. The six policy documents are made up and the scheduling tool is a mock, so nothing connects to a real clinic system or real patient data.
    • Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a standard public pattern. ChromaDB, sentence-transformers and the hosted Groq model are existing tools that I configured and connected, not things I trained.
    • There is no live demo. The code is public, and the service runs locally or in Docker.

    How it is built

    How the chatbot finds and cites an answer

    1. 01Questiontyped into the chat screen
    2. 02Routerbooking, small talk or policy
    3. 03Searchtop 3 passages from ChromaDB
    4. 04Score checkweak matches dropped, no AI call
    5. 05Strict promptanswer only from passages
    6. 06Cited replysource, excerpt, confidence label
    1. 01

      Six made-up policy documents (appointments, discharge, health-privacy rules, insurance, refills and telehealth) are read from plain text files and cut into chunks of 500 characters with an overlap of 50

    2. 02

      Meaning-based search: sentence-transformers turns each chunk into numbers, stored in ChromaDB on disk. The model runs on the CPU and is baked into the Docker image, and the top 3 matches are used

    3. 03

      A three-way router based on keywords: booking requests go to a mock scheduling tool, small talk to a friendly persona and everything else to the document chatbot

    4. 04

      A similarity score threshold, set in the settings, drops weak matches before the AI is called. If nothing passes, a fixed "not found" reply is returned and the AI is not called at all

    5. 05

      A strict instruction with an exact fallback sentence. Each answer drawn from the documents returns the source document, a short excerpt of the passage and a confidence label based on how many documents matched

    6. 06

      A chain of backup Groq models: if one fails or is retired, the next is tried and the reply names the model that answered. Prompts and settings live in plain files

    7. 07

      A FastAPI backend with health, ingest and ask endpoints, input limits and a request ID on the log lines of each request, a Streamlit chat screen and Docker packaging

    Decisions

    Design decisions and trade-offs

    • Drop weak matches before the AI

      ChoiceI filter retrieved passages by a similarity score and return a fixed "not found" reply, without calling the AI, when none pass.

      WhyThe AI cannot misuse a passage it never sees, and an out-of-scope question costs no AI call. The threshold is a setting, so it can be made stricter without touching code.

    • Send unclear questions to the documents

      ChoiceI used plain keyword rules to separate booking, small talk and policy questions. Anything unclear goes to document search, not to the chat persona.

      WhyRules are easy to read and test. A misrouted policy question still meets the score threshold and the strict prompt, which is safer than a friendly reply that invents facts.

    • Keep prompts and settings in files

      ChoicePrompt wording sits in plain text files, and the main tunable values (models, threshold, chunk size, input limits, which websites may call the API) sit in settings, not in the code.

      WhyA reviewer who does not write code can read the prompt, changes show up as plain text differences, and tuning or swapping a model does not mean editing the code.

    • A chain of backup models

      ChoiceI made the model list a setting. The service tries the main Groq model, then each backup in order, and every reply from the model names the model that answered.

      WhyHosted models get retired. The settings file itself records two of the models I used as deprecated, and a backup list lets the service keep answering while I update it.

    What I tried first, and what changed

    First attempt. My first version passed the top 3 passages to the AI whatever their score and relied on the prompt alone to refuse. Later I limited document search to questions containing a listed healthcare word, so anything else fell through to a canned greeting.

    What changed. I added a score threshold so weak matches never reach the AI, and made document search the default again for anything that is not a booking request or small talk. I then replaced the canned greetings with a persona prompt that may not invent facility facts.

    Results

    Saying "not found" instead of guessing

    Every number comes with how it was measured. The limits are part of the result.

    Results

    • Each answer drawn from the documents returns its source document, a short excerpt of the passage used and a confidence label
    • Out-of-scope questions are meant to get a fixed fallback sentence, not a guess: weak matches never reach the AI, and the strict prompt asks it to use the same sentence when the passages do not answer
    • The service keeps running when a hosted model is retired, without a code change, because the backup models are a setting
    • 6 quick automated smoke tests check the health endpoint, input validation and document loading, and the backend is packaged in Docker
    • Each request gets a short ID that appears in its log lines and in the X-Request-ID response header, so one question can be followed through the logs
    • Re-loading unchanged documents does not create duplicate chunks, because each chunk gets a fixed ID built from its file name and text

    Limits to keep in mind

    • It reads only plain text files, runs on six made-up documents and has not been tried on real policy sets. It does not reload documents when a file changes, and old chunks stay until the collection is reset.
    • The repository keeps no scored check of answer quality. The 12 sample questions (plus 2 out-of-scope ones) are a manual list with expected answers, and the confidence label comes from how many documents matched. It is not a probability.
    • Routing uses keyword rules that can misread unusual wording. The booking tool is a mock, and there is no sign-in, no memory between questions and only a single language.
    • Questions and the few passages used are sent to a hosted AI service. A real deployment would need your own documents, a privacy and legal review, secured hosting and probably a private model.

    In a business

    Where this kind of solution fits

    Situations where a team could use the same approach. These are examples, not past clients.

    • Clinics and hospital groups

      Front-desk staff and patients ask about refills, discharge limits, privacy and billing. A cited assistant answers from the facility's own policies and sends booking requests onward.

    • Insurers and claims teams

      Policy holders ask what a plan covers or how to dispute a bill. The assistant answers from the plan documents and shows the passage behind each answer.

    • HR and internal policy desks

      Staff ask about leave, expenses or conduct rules. An assistant that cites the handbook and admits when the handbook is silent is meant to cut down repeat questions to HR.

    • Banks and regulated firms

      Staff must follow internal procedures and regulatory manuals. An assistant that answers with a source passage, or says "not found", lets a reviewer check every answer.

    FAQ

    Questions about this chatbot

    How do you build a RAG chatbot?

    Cut your documents into chunks, turn each chunk into numbers a search index can compare, and store them. When a question arrives, find the best matching chunks, then ask an AI model to answer only from them. Here that used chunks of 500 with an overlap of 50, ChromaDB and the top 3 matches.

    How do you evaluate a RAG chatbot?

    Check three things. Does each answer cite the right source? Do out-of-scope questions get a plain "not found"? Are weak matches dropped before the AI is called? This prototype has 6 quick automated tests and a manual list of sample questions, but no scored check of answer quality. I would add that first for a client.

    What happens when the answer is not in the documents?

    The chatbot says so. Weak matches are dropped before the AI is called, and the AI is told to use an exact fallback sentence when nothing relevant remains. That is how an out-of-scope question is meant to get a plain "not found" reply, pointing the person to the facility, instead of a confident guess.

    Does it use real patient data?

    No. It is a prototype built on six made-up policy documents and uses no real patient data. A real deployment would need your own documents, encrypted and access-controlled hosting, a privacy and legal review, agreements with any cloud provider that sees the data, and proper testing with the staff who will use it.

    Can this chatbot book real appointments?

    No. The scheduling tool is a mock. It picks a department and a day out of the question and returns four example time slots, marked as illustrative, with a note to call or use the patient portal to confirm. Real booking would mean connecting it to your own scheduling system, which I can build.

    Does the chatbot remember earlier questions in a chat?

    No. The chat screen shows the conversation so far, but the backend answers each question on its own, so a follow-up such as "and what about children?" carries no context. Adding memory is the improvement I listed for this project, and it needs care so old answers do not mix with new passages.

    What does the chatbot send to the hosted AI service?

    At question time, only the last step goes online. The model that searches the documents runs on the server's own processor. For each question, the question text and the few matching passages are sent to the hosted Groq model, which writes the answer. For private data you would use a model on your own servers.
