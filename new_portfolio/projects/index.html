    How to read them

    What each case study contains

    1. 01

      Why it matters

      Each case study opens with the business need and the cost of the problem, before any technology is named, so you can tell quickly whether it resembles your situation.

    2. 02

      My role in the build

      Each case study lists what I did myself and what already existed, such as teammates' work, public recipes or earlier code, so you can judge my own part fairly.

    3. 03

      Decisions and trade-offs

      Each decision says what I chose, where there was a real alternative, and why. This is the best sign of how I would reason about your project.

    4. 04

      Results and how measured

      Every accuracy figure says what it was measured on, such as a random test split or queries run on a real database. Caveats are stated next to the number.

    5. 05

      Limits and where else it fits

      Limits say plainly where the work stops. Use cases and extensions then show where the same approach could fit and what I can add for you.

    FAQ

    About these projects

    What are some real world AI project examples?

    Real examples on this site include an AI that drafts audit findings from checklist notes, a text-to-SQL assistant for a bank database and a scam-catching AI honeypot. Each one has a case study with the problem, the build and the results.

    Which projects are client work and which are personal builds?

    The audit drafting, obligation extraction and text-to-SQL case studies are client work at Claptek, and the WhatsApp platform was a client build during an internship. The others are personal or academic builds, including hackathon and job-application work. Every case study states its origin and my role, so you can see how much of it is mine.

    Why are the client projects described without names?

    Client work is confidential. The pages explain the problem, my part and the measured results without naming the client, the product or any internal system. I only name a client or show their work with written approval. Anything I cannot share, or cannot prove, stays off the page.

    Can I see the code behind these projects?

    Where a repository is public, the case study links to it. That includes the healthcare chatbot, the honeypot, the CCTV prototype and the speech, deepfake and spam builds. Client code and the traceable backend are private, so for those I explain the design, my part and the results instead.

    How do you separate what you built from what you adapted?

    Each case study lists what I did myself next to what already existed, such as a teammate's work, a public training recipe or earlier code. The speech model adapts a public recipe, and in the text-to-SQL work my teammate built the table search and the safety guard.

    Are the accuracy numbers on these pages from real tests?

    Yes. Each accuracy figure states how it was measured, for example a random test split, 1,034 test messages or queries run on a real database. Where a score is modest or has a caveat, the page says so next to the number. I do not add figures I cannot show.
